### Overview

AI coding assistants started as smarter autocomplete. Simpson Strong-Tie, a 6,500-person manufacturer of structural building products, is moving past that. The company is retiring GitHub Copilot and moving 200 developers and QA engineers to Claude, with spec-driven development at the center. Specs come first, and Claude works from them.

Bill Wolff, software architect and Microsoft MVP, covers why the company made the switch, how the rollout is being run, and what spec-driven development looks like in daily work. Then he goes live, walking through the 5–10 projects he juggles at once: greenfield cloud-native apps, migrations off aging desktop technology, Azure Monitor workbooks, and Azure deployments. Each is backed by a documentation trail across GitHub, Confluence, Jira, and draw.io. You’ll see how specs, tickets, and diagrams give Claude the context it needs to do real work.

The session closes with a candid, off-the-slides discussion of results so far. Bring your questions.

## Bill Wolff

Bill Wolff has spent decades deep in the Microsoft ecosystem and brings real-world experience you don't get from slides alone. He is an independent consultant, trainer, and architect with Agility Systems. Over the years, he has led SharePoint and Microsoft practices at Capax Global, Unisys, and LiquidHub. He also ran his own consulting firm, Wolff Data Systems, for 15 years—directing armies of consultants back in the dot-com era and learning a thing or two along the way.

Beyond consulting, Bill is a true community builder. He's the founder and President of the philly.NET user group, a former INETA board member and Vice President of the Speaker Bureau, a contributing author on several books, and a Microsoft MVP with certifications as a trainer, systems engineer, and developer.
